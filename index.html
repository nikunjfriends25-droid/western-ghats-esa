About this atlas

On 31 July 2024 the Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change published draft notification S.O. 3060(E), listing 4,402 villages across Goa, Gujarat, Karnataka, Kerala, Maharashtra and Tamil Nadu proposed as an Ecologically Sensitive Area in the Western Ghats. The notification names those villages in a table. It does not publish their boundaries.

This atlas reconstructs those boundaries. Every village named in Annexure C was matched to its polygon in the all-India census village boundary dataset, giving 4,331 of 4,402 rows (98.4%) a mapped extent carrying its 2011 Census code.

How far can you trust it?

Three independent checks, because a village-name match can be confidently wrong.

  1. Against the gazette's own coordinates. Annexure B lists 401 points on the ESA's outer boundary. Measured against the reconstructed boundary, the median distance is 101 m in Goa, 244 m in Gujarat and 536 m in Karnataka. These are boundary points, so the test is distance to the polygon edge, not whether they fall inside.
  2. Against Kerala's own published ESA. Kerala released its ESA as KML. 98.1% of that official area falls inside these polygons.
  3. Against the notified areas. Annexure A states an area per state. The reconstruction totals 56,254 km² against a notified 56,826 km² — a ratio of 0.99.

Where it is weakest

Two limits matter more than the headline numbers.

Bifurcated villages are drawn whole. The gazette marks some villages with an asterisk to mean only part of the village falls inside the ESA. No partial extent is published, so those polygons show the entire village and over-state the area. Only the State Governments' physical-verification maps can resolve them.

Kerala is portion-wise by design. Kerala defines its ESA as portions of villages, which is why its ratio here is 1.30 rather than 1.00. For Kerala, treat the official state KML layer as authoritative and these village polygons as context.

Karnataka is the least complete state at 0.86 of its notified area.

This is an independent reconstruction for reference and research. It is not an official record, has no legal standing, and must not be used to determine whether a specific parcel of land falls inside the ESA. For that, consult the notification itself and the relevant State Government.

Using the data programmatically

The API is a tree of static JSON files served over HTTPS with Access-Control-Allow-Origin: *, so you can call it from a browser, a notebook or another site with no key and no rate limit. It is read-only and addressed by path — there are no query parameters, because there is no server.

EndpointReturns
api/v1/summary.jsonPer-state counts, areas and notified comparison
api/v1/states/{CODE}.jsonEvery village in a state, attributes only. Codes: GA GJ KA KL MH TN
api/v1/villages/{censuscode}.jsonOne village as a GeoJSON Feature, full resolution
api/v1/boundary-points.jsonThe 401 Annexure B boundary points
